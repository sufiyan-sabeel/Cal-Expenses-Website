Privacy

Privacy Policy

Applies to the Cal Expenses Android application and this website. Last updated: 2026.

In one line: Cal Expenses keeps your financial data on your device. There is no Cal Expenses account, no Cal Expenses server, no analytics and no ads. The only feature that sends anything off your device is the optional Cal AI assistant, and only when you choose to use it.

1. What the app stores on your device

When you use Cal Expenses, the following is stored in a private database inside the app on your device:

  • Transactions you enter — amount, type, category, title and date/time.
  • Budgets and savings goals you create.
  • Voice notes you record, including audio files saved in the app's private storage, and any text you attach.
  • Tasks, checklists, due dates and reminders.
  • Your preferences — theme, currency and app settings.
  • Cal AI conversation history, if you use the assistant.

This data is held in the app's private storage area. Other apps on your device cannot read it without root access.

2. What leaves your device

For the core features listed above, nothing is transmitted anywhere. The app has no Cal Expenses backend, so there is no server that could receive your financial records, and the app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking code.

Two things can leave your device, and only through your own deliberate action:

  • Files you export. JSON backups, CSV exports and PDF reports are written to a location you pick through Android's file picker. Where they go — and who can see them — is entirely your choice.
  • The optional Cal AI assistant. Described next.

3. The Cal AI assistant (optional)

Cal AI is switched off until you add your own API key. When you use it:

  • The text of the message you type is sent to OpenRouter (openrouter.ai) so a language model can produce a reply.
  • If — and only if — you turn on the "Include my spending summary" switch, a short summary is also sent: your totals for the current month, your top expense categories, and your budget figures. The switch is off by default.
  • Your OpenRouter API key is stored encrypted on your device using the Android Keystore, and is never placed in the app package.
  • Only the most recent turns of the current conversation are sent, not your whole database or all of your chat history.
  • OpenRouter's own terms and privacy policy govern what it does with a request once it reaches them. Free models on OpenRouter may have different data policies from paid ones — check the model's listing before sending anything sensitive.

If you never set a key, or never open the assistant, no request is ever made.

4. Permissions the app requests

  • Microphone — only when you record a voice note or dictate text. Asked for at that moment, not at startup.
  • Notifications — only so task and budget reminders can be shown.
  • Internet — needed solely for the optional Cal AI assistant.

No other permissions are requested. The app does not request access to your contacts, location, camera, or your files at large.

5. Backups and exports

Backup and export files are plain, unencrypted files created at your request and saved where you choose. Treat them as you would any document containing your financial information — if you store one in a shared or cloud-synced folder, whoever can access that folder can read it. Importing a backup replaces the app's current data, so the app asks for confirmation and validates the file first.

6. Deleting your data

  • Delete individual transactions, budgets, goals, notes and tasks inside the app.
  • Clear Cal AI chat history from Settings.
  • Uninstall the app to remove its local database and any recordings it stored.

Because there is no Cal Expenses account or server, uninstalling is complete — there is no copy of your financial data held anywhere else by us.

7. Children

Cal Expenses is a general-purpose personal finance tool and is not directed at children.

8. This website

This is a static site hosted on GitHub Pages. It contains no analytics, no cookies set by us and no tracking scripts. GitHub may collect standard technical information such as IP addresses as part of serving the site; see GitHub's own privacy statement for that.

9. Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change — for example, if a new online feature is added — this page will be updated and the date above changed.

10. Contact

Questions about privacy, or about how the app handles your data? Open an issue on GitHub.
